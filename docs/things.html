Things you can make

Press New thing, pick a category, then the thing. Each page says what it is and what you can set.

Blocks and items#

  • Area value: A number the world keeps by place, not by player or item: an aura in each chunk, an oil reservoir under it, radiation, a corruption that spreads, a pollution.
  • Armor: Something worn that protects.
  • Assembly station: A block with a screen where a player fits, tunes, mends, names and takes apart the tool in their hand.
  • Backpack: An item with slots of its own, opened from the hand.
  • Bag of pieces: A bag that keeps the small pieces a carving tool takes away, by look, and gives them back when pieces are added.
  • Banner pattern: A pattern woven on a banner in the loom, and shown on a shield.
  • Battery: An item that keeps energy: charged in a machine, it charges the tools and armour a player carries, and powers a machine it is put in.
  • Block: Something placed in the world: ore, stone, a crate.
  • Boat: A boat or a raft a player rows on water, with a chest or not.
  • Bow: The game's own bow, drawn and let go, to change: how long it draws, how hard and how fast what it shoots flies.
  • Building plan: An item that copies the blocks between two corners and builds the copy again wherever it is used, a few blocks a second, from the player's items.
  • Building tool: Places a square of blocks at once where it is used, a ghost of them shown first.
  • Building wand: A wand that lays a line, a wall or a box of blocks from a corner a player marks to the block it is used on, outlining it first.
  • Capsule: An item that takes a box of blocks round the block it is used on, chests with their items too, and places it back wherever it is used next, showing a ghost first.
  • Carved block: A block made of small pieces of any block's look, carved on its Carve tab or by players in the world.
  • Carving tool: A chisel or any tool that carves small pieces out of blocks or adds them, in one piece or a shape: a line, a flat square, a cube, a ball and more.
  • Copy wand: A wand that copies the box between two corners and pastes it wherever it is used, outlining the paste first.
  • Crate: An item that opens into a loot table when used, made with its table.
  • Crop: A plant that grows stage by stage on farmland from its own seeds, as wheat, and gives what it grows when it is ripe.
  • Crossbow: The game's own crossbow, loaded and then fired, to change.
  • Designator: An item the player uses on a block to remember a place, then on an aiming block to give it that place as its target.
  • Fishing rod: The game's own fishing rod: cast into water and reeled in with a catch.
  • Food: Something eaten.
  • Forms for every material: Makes a dust, a plate, a gear or any other form for every metal and gem the game has when it starts, other mods too.
  • Fuel: Something a furnace burns.
  • Gene: A trait any thing can carry and pass to its young: a number such as Growth, or one of a few choices such as Coat colour.
  • Hat: Something worn on the head, drawn on the wearer from its own picture.
  • Item: Something held in the hand or kept in a chest.
  • Letter: An item that holds a few things and is mailed to a player.
  • Material: What parts are cut from, any item the game's iron ingot among them: its color, and the numbers and abilities it gives a part.
  • Mob catcher: An item that catches a creature with its name, health and everything it is, and lets it out again where it is used.
  • Module: Something installed into a tool or armour that takes modules, switching an ability on and spending its energy.
  • Music disc: A disc that plays a song in a jukebox, the game's or your own, its words shown as it starts.
  • Ore: A block that appears in the world in veins.
  • Ore with a pickaxe: An ore that needs an iron pickaxe, its ingot and a pickaxe.
  • Part: A part a tool is assembled from, cut from any material in the stonecutter, wearing its material's color.
  • Plant: A flower or small plant that stands on grass, as a poppy.
  • Pocket: Put into a backpack in the crafting grid, it adds nine slots.
  • Pointer: An item whose needle turns toward something: a transmitter on a channel, a mob you tagged, or the nearest block of a kind.
  • Powered armour: A piece of armour that runs on energy: each hit its wearer takes spends some, and empty it guards nothing until it is charged.
  • Powered tool: A tool and weapon that runs on energy instead of wearing out: each block and each hit spends some, and empty it mines as a hand.
  • Satellite: A launched thing that watches a place and reports back.
  • Set: Makes an ore, its ingot, tools and armor, with recipes.
  • Settings copier: Copies one block's settings and gives them to another of the same kind.
  • Shape pattern: An item that copies the shape of a carved block and prints it again on another block.
  • Shears: The game's own shears: they shear sheep and cut leaves, cobwebs and wool.
  • Shield: The game's own shield, raised to stop a hit, to change.
  • Space station: A built place in a dimension of its own that anyone or any vehicle docks with, orbiting a world at a height that rules move.
  • Species: A kind of bee, plant or animal: the gene values it starts with, what it looks like as a block, an item or a mob, and which two species bred give it.
  • Spell: A spell of its own: what it does as rules (when it is cast, when it reaches a mob or a block, every tick it is kept up), what it costs (mana or any value), its cooldown and charge-up, and how it reaches: on the caster, along a ray, as a missile or all around.
  • Spell book: An item that teaches the spells listed on it when it is used, so a wand that casts the spells a player knows can cast them.
  • Spell scroll: An item that casts its spell once and is used up.
  • Spell wand: An item that casts spells: the ones listed on it, or every spell of a kit the player has learned.
  • Thrown weapon: Something thrown that flies as itself and hits: a throwing star, a javelin.
  • Tool: A pickaxe, axe or shovel, or a hammer that breaks a square at once.
  • Tool for everything: One tool that mines as a pickaxe, an axe, a shovel and a hoe at once.
  • Tool of parts: A tool assembled from parts in the crafting grid, its numbers and abilities its materials'; a part laid beside it swaps in.
  • Tree axe: An axe that fells a whole tree from one log, outlining the tree while it is aimed at.
  • Value: A number or a list kept by an item, the player, a team or the world: Energy, XP, Kills, the foods a player has eaten.
  • Vehicle: Something to ride that is not alive, beyond the boat: a cart, a motor boat, a broom or an airship, a minecart of its own, a ride along a path.
  • Vehicle part: An item put into a vehicle by using it on it, that works there every so often: breaks the blocks ahead, harvests crops, lights the way, shoots, blows up, or what its own rules say.
  • Vein pickaxe: A pickaxe that mines a whole joined vein of ore from one block, outlining the vein while it is aimed at.
  • Waypoint compass: An item a player marks places of their own with, lists them and travels to them, each shown on a bar along the top of the screen.
  • Weapon: A sword, an axe, or something new that hits.
  • World event: Something that happens to a whole dimension for a while: a blood sky, a fog bank, an aurora, a rift.

Crafting and loot#

  • Armor trim: A trim put on armor at the smithing table, in any of the game's materials.
  • Condition: A test in plain words, like half of the time, only in a biome, only at night.
  • Drop: What falls out when a block breaks or a mob dies.
  • Effect: Something that happens to a mob for a while.
  • Enchantment: A power added to a tool or weapon.
  • Extras for an item: What any item, the game's or yours, also does: composts, burns in a furnace, or what a dispenser does with it.
  • Game thing changed: One of the game's own blocks, items or mobs given new values, such as a block's hardness, an item's stack size or a mob's health, a structure the game makes no more, or a limit the game sets, such as the anvil's most cost.
  • Item change: What happens to an item after it is made: its count, its wear, a name, a lore line, a random enchantment.
  • Loot changed: What the game's own blocks, mobs and chests drop, changed in place: things taken out, or one thing given in place of another.
  • Loot table: A weighted list of what can come out, rolled by a quest, a crate, a mob's or a block's drop, or a rule.
  • Machine recipe: What one machine of yours turns into something else, and what it costs.
  • Other mod's recipe: A recipe for a machine from a mod yours builds on, filled in by clicks.
  • Potion: A drink that gives an effect.
  • Recipe: Make something in a crafting table, furnace, stonecutter or smithing table.
  • Recipe change: Change a recipe the game or another mod already has.
  • Recipes taken away: Take recipes the game has out of the game.
  • Ritual: Offerings on pedestals around an altar, a catalyst on the altar, the conditions it asks, and the result it leaves on the altar.
  • Station recipe: A recipe done on a station's own screen, with cells, tanks and energy.
  • Villager trade: What a villager offers and asks for.

Machines and networks#

  • Access point: A block that gives wireless items a range to its network.
  • Aiming block: A block that keeps a target place: a launcher's, a gate's other end.
  • Altar: A block that performs rituals with offerings on pedestals around it, formed while a ring of gold blocks lies around it; say in its rules what forming does: summon, teleport, give, open a portal.
  • Asking chest: A chest that keeps itself stocked from the storage network or by couriers.
  • Cable: Carries energy between machines.
  • Chemicals: The chemicals kind, ready made: gases and other chemicals that tanks hold and pipes carry beside energy, fluid and items.
  • Chest: A block that holds items and opens a screen you lay out.
  • Configurator: A tool that sets the faces of a placed block: right-click a face to step its mode, sneak to go back.
  • Crafter: Holds patterns, so the terminal can craft what they make.
  • Crafting station: A block with a 3x3 grid and a result, like a crafting table.
  • Custom kind: A matter of your own beside energy and fluid: a chemical, mana, heat, whatever your mod calls it.
  • Dense cable: A storage cable that carries 32 channels instead of 8.
  • Disk for fluids: A storage disk that holds fluids by the bucket's thousandths.
  • Drawer: A block that holds many of one item, shown on its front.
  • Drive: A block whose slots hold storage disks.
  • Electric furnace: A machine that cooks sand into glass using energy.
  • Face part: An item placed on a face of a block that holds parts: it takes in, sends out, places, breaks, counts, shows or covers.
  • Fluid: A liquid that flows in the world and fills a bucket, as water does.
  • Fluid tank: A tank that holds 8000 mB and opens its own screen.
  • Formed machine: A machine built of many blocks: it works only while the blocks around it stand as its pattern, drawn layer by layer on its Pattern tab, says.
  • Generator: Burns something and makes energy, on a recipe you write.
  • Generator with fuel: A generator that burns coal into energy, ready to run.
  • Item filter: A filter as an item: marked in the filter of a pipe's face, that face lets through what its rules say, items and tags, mods, wear and enchantment.
  • Item pipe: Carries items between chests and machines.
  • Link: A block that joins the chests beside it to every other link in range, with no cable: a spark or a crystal.
  • Machine: A block with an inventory, a tank or power.
  • Machine casing: A part of a machine built of many blocks: pipes and cables reach the machine through it, using it opens the machine's screen, and while the machine stands its state "formed" is on, so it may look different then (give that look a picture, or an empty one to hide it under the machine's own model).
  • Mailbox: Sends letters and keeps each player's post.
  • Matter pipe: A pipe that carries a matter of your own from the blocks that give it to the blocks that take it, holding none itself.
  • Matter tank: A block that holds a matter of your own and joins pipes of it on every side: a chemical tank, a mana pool, a store of heat.
  • Network heart: The block a storage network can need to work.
  • Pattern table: Turns a blank pattern into a recipe for the crafter.
  • Pedestal: A block that holds one item put on it by hand and shows it floating above it; an altar's rituals take their offerings from the pedestals around it.
  • Pipe: Carries fluid between tanks and machines.
  • Powered machine: A machine with a recipe that uses energy and time.
  • Receiver: A block that gives out the redstone sent on its channel, by a transmitter or by a rule.
  • Shared chest: A chest that is the same chest wherever it is placed: every shared chest on one channel holds the same items, in every world.
  • Shared tank: A tank that is the same tank wherever it is placed: every shared tank on one channel holds the same fluid, in every world, kept even while none of them is loaded.
  • Storage cable: Joins cells, drawers, chests and terminals into one network.
  • Storage cell: A block that holds many kinds of item, joined by cables.
  • Storage crate: A box that keeps what it holds when broken: carry it away full.
  • Storage disk: An item that holds many kinds of item by count.
  • Tank: Holds a fluid and gives it to whatever asks for it.
  • Terminal: Lists what the network holds, to take and put by clicks.
  • Trade station: Gives what it offers for what it wants, from its own slots.
  • Transmitter: A block that sends the redstone reaching it on a channel, to every receiver on that channel, however far away.
  • Upgrade: An item a player uses on a placed block that takes upgrades: it goes in and makes the block faster, bigger, quicker to move things or cheaper to run.
  • When fluids meet: What happens where a fluid meets another fluid or a block: lava meeting water makes obsidian, your own fluid meeting lava makes a block or drops an item.
  • Wireless energy node: A block that sends the energy its cables bring on a channel, or, set to receive, gives out on its cables the energy the channel brings.
  • Wireless terminal: An item that opens a terminal's screen from far away, while in its range.

Mobs and models#

  • Animation: How a model moves over time.
  • Attribute: A number every living thing has, as luck and armor are.
  • Boss: A hostile mob with a boss bar, special moves and a phase it enters when it is badly hurt.
  • Damage change: A hurt a living thing takes, changed or cancelled by who takes it, who deals it and what it is, on every game version, without code of yours inside the game's own.
  • Flying mount: A mount that flies with its rider, beating its wings on each jump.
  • Golem-style creature: A creature built of parts and materials, as a golem is: used on a block it becomes the creature, its health, speed, armor, jobs and look its parts'.
  • Mob: A living thing with a model, animations and behavior.
  • Mob behaviour: A piece of your own code that joins one activity of a mob's brain, the game's or another mod's.
  • Model: The shape of a block, item or mob, made from boxes.
  • Mount: A mob tamed by riding it until it calms, then saddled, ridden and given bags and armour.
  • Movement: How a living thing moves, the player or a mob: its speed and its jump, and a class of yours run as it moves.
  • Painting: A picture hung on a wall.
  • Particle: Sparks, smoke, dust and the like.
  • Pet: A mob a player tames with an item; it sits, follows and fights for them.
  • Player animation: How a player's body moves for a moment, a wave or a bow, played on a player by a rule.
  • Projectile: A thing that flies, drawn by a model: a spell, a dart, a boomerang, a homing missile.
  • Sea mount: A swimming mob ridden under water, its rider breathing.
  • Skin: A player's skin: the body and its picture to paint, with poses to show it in.
  • Sound: A sound the game plays.
  • Texture: The pixels a block, item or mob wears.
  • The player: The game's own player: its body, and rules for its moments, such as when it eats, is hurt or joins.
  • Thing: Something in the world that is not a mob and does not fly: a spell's ward, a frame on a wall, a floating orb, a balloon on a string, a trap.
  • Villager job: A job a villager takes at its workstation, with its coat and its trades.
  • Worn slots: Slots you name on any mob, the game's own or yours: a hat on a cow, boots on its four legs, a saddle on a pig, armor on a wolf.

Logic and code#

  • Access transformer (NeoForge): Opens members the game keeps private, on NeoForge.
  • Access widener (Fabric): Opens members the game keeps private, on Fabric, in the game's own names.
  • Another mod: A mod this one uses or works with, such as a recipe viewer: built against, in the test game, and named in both manifests.
  • Behavior: What happens when something happens: when this sword hits, set the target on fire.
  • Code: A Java class of your own, kept safe when the mod is rebuilt.
  • Command: A command typed in chat with a slash, whose rules run when a player types it.
  • Commands to run: A list of the game's commands run together by name, when a world loads, or every tick.
  • Damage type: A way of getting hurt, with its own message.
  • Dimension file of your own: A dimension written by hand as the game's own data file, which a biome source of your own code can fill.
  • File of your own: Any file the mod ships as it is, under resources/: data, assets, anything the game reads.
  • Game rule: A rule of the world, set with the gamerule command.
  • Locks: What a player may not use, wear, break, place, hit, enter or ride until they reach a skill level, a value or an advancement.
  • Look of a block made in code: The blockstate, models, loot table and name a block registered in your own code needs.
  • Mixin: A piece of your own code that changes one of the game's classes, listed in your mixin config for you.
  • Mixin config: Your own mixins: notes placed inside the game's code.
  • Mod settings: A setting players can change after the mod is built, in its settings file: a number, on or off, one word out of a list, or a list of entries they edit.
  • Pack file: One file of a data pack or a resource pack, in any folder the game reads, yours or the game's changed.
  • Tag: A named group of blocks or items other rules can point at.
  • Translation: The names of your things in another language.
  • Word of your own: A few lines you name once and use in any rule as one line: it is given as many things as it needs, and it can give back an answer.

The world#

  • Berry bush: A block that ripens in a minute in the light and gives berries when a player picks it, then grows again.
  • Biome: A region of the world with its own look and life.
  • Caves: Caves, ravines or Nether tunnels the world cuts into its ground.
  • Dimension: A whole other world reached through a portal.
  • Feature: Something the world grows as it is made: a patch of flowers, a tree, a boulder, a lake, a vein of a block.
  • Structure: A building or ruin placed in the world.
  • Structure finder: An item that finds the nearest of the structures it names and shows the way: an eye for a dungeon, a map stone for ruins.
  • Waypoint: A block that marks where it stands by a name, for rules to send players there: a warp stone, a home, a town's gate.
  • World type: A choice on the game's Create New World screen.

Screens and guides#

  • Advancement: One of the game's own advancements: a goal with a toast, reached by an item, a kill or a rule.
  • Chest menu: A chest's screen of items, one to six rows of nine, opened by a rule for one player; each item runs its own lines when clicked, and nothing can be taken from it.
  • Component: A small layout placed inside other screens as one control: a card, a row of a list, a header.
  • Creative tab: A tab of the creative inventory with the things you put in it, or your things added to one of the game's tabs.
  • Drawing in the world: Drawn in the world from what the player looks at: an outline, the vein a key will break, a beam from the hand.
  • Guide book: A book of pages about the mod: words, pictures, items and recipes.
  • HUD drawing in code: Your own code that draws on the HUD every frame, in a box you place on the screen.
  • HUD layout: A whole screen layout you drag together: bars, words and pictures kept to a corner or an edge.
  • HUD number bar: One number of the player shown over the game as a bar or words, like saturation above the hunger bar.
  • Key binding: A key players press to do something of yours.
  • Quests: Chapters of quests a player finishes for rewards, read in a book of the mod's own, each quest placed on its chapter's page.
  • Research: Studies a player researches with items, levels and time, each unlocking recipes and more.
  • Scene: A short in-game demonstration of how something works.
  • Screen: A screen of the mod's own, laid out by dragging: opened by a rule, or shown always over the game.
  • Skill: A skill with levels, like Mining or Farming: XP comes from any moment, each level reached runs its rewards, and locks may wait for a level.
  • Skill tree: Skills a player buys with points, each after the ones it needs.
  • Tooltip lines: Lines added to the tooltip of any item or block, the mod's or the game's, or of every item, each filled in from the item's numbers and values and shown by its own condition.

Project kinds#

  • Mod: Blocks, items, recipes and everything else, built for NeoForge and Fabric.
  • Data pack: Recipes, drops, tags and world data with no code, for a plain game.
  • Resource pack: Textures, models, sounds and translations for the game's own things.
  • Modpack: A collection of published mods, yours and other people's, ready to install.
